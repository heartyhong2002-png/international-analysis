  <!-- 4. Section 1: BACKGROUND AND SCOPE -->
  <h2 style="font-size: 15px; font-weight: 900; color: #0F172A; border-bottom: 1.5px solid #CBD5E1; padding-bottom: 4px; margin: 26px 0 10px 0; letter-spacing: 0.5px; text-transform: uppercase;">
    1. BACKGROUND AND SCOPE
  </h2>
  <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px;">
    Russia's invasion of Ukraine, which began in February 2022, has continued for more than four years into 2025-2026; despite active mediation by the Trump administration, repeated rounds of negotiation have stalled over territory, security guarantees, and NATO membership, producing a pattern of a structurally frozen war.
  </p>
  <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <strong>Impact of the Trump Administration:</strong> The Trump administration initially scaled back direct grant aid to Ukraine and pressed for a minerals deal, but from July 2025 shifted to a NATO-funded arms-sale model (PURL) that shifted costs to European allies while actually sustaining and expanding weapons deliveries to Ukraine.
  </p>

  <h2 style="font-size: 15px; font-weight: 900; color: #0F172A; border-bottom: 1.5px solid #CBD5E1; padding-bottom: 4px; margin: 26px 0 10px 0; letter-spacing: 0.5px; text-transform: uppercase;">
    3. ECONOMIC AND SECURITY IMPACT ANALYSIS
  </h2>
  
  <div style="margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Security & Defense Dimension</div>
    <ul style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 4px 0 8px 20px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>North Korea & Regional Security:</strong> Following deployment to the Kursk front, North Korea faces reports of a possible additional 30,000-troop deployment for frontline duty in exchange for cash, food, fuel, and reportedly military technology and WMD-related cooperation from Russia -- a direct concern for the Korean Peninsula's military balance.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Alliance Impact:</strong> North Korean troops gaining combat experience in Ukraine and deepening North Korea-Russia military cooperation underscore the need for strengthened ROK-US and trilateral ROK-US-Japan security cooperation.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Military & Missile Threats:</strong> Security experts warn that if North Korea acquires missile and drone technology or combat data through its Russia cooperation, this could sophisticate the missile threat it poses to South Korea.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div style="margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Economic, Trade & Financial Dimension</div>
    <ul style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 4px 0 8px 20px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Trade & Tariffs:</strong> Prolonged sanctions have kept South Korea's direct trade with Russia limited, while defense contracts with European countries like Poland (K2 tanks, K9 howitzers) have expanded, alongside competition among Korean construction and infrastructure firms to secure positions in Ukraine's reconstruction market.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Foreign Exchange & Currency:</strong> The Ukraine war itself has a secondary effect on the won-dollar exchange rate compared to factors like Middle East tensions, but periodic safe-haven-driven won depreciation pressure has appeared during phases of heightened geopolitical risk.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Cross-Border Investment:</strong> Korean defense stocks such as Hanwha Aerospace and Hyundai Rotem have drawn sustained investor interest as beneficiaries of Europe's security realignment, buoyed by large-scale Poland orders.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Public Opinion & Expert Consensus</div>
    <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 0 0 4px 0;"><strong>Public Sentiment:</strong> Surveys show 64% of Russians and 66% of Ukrainians support a negotiated end to the war, indicating growing war-weariness on both sides.</p>
    <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 0;"><strong>Expert Assessment:</strong> Former US envoy Kurt Volker assessed that Russia is 'not negotiating in good faith,' and most experts judge that fundamental disagreements over territory and security guarantees make a breakthrough within 2026 unrealistic, with the conflict likely to prolong into an effectively frozen state.</p>
  </div>

  <!-- 7. Section 4: RISK OUTLOOK AND UNCERTAINTIES -->
  <h2 style="font-size: 15px; font-weight: 900; color: #0F172A; border-bottom: 1.5px solid #CBD5E1; padding-bottom: 4px; margin: 26px 0 10px 0; letter-spacing: 0.5px; text-transform: uppercase;">
    4. PERFORMANCE BENCHMARKS AND GAP ANALYSIS
  </h2>
  <div style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin-bottom: 12px;">
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Short Term (3-6 months):</strong> Over the coming months, partial and temporary truces are likely to be attempted repeatedly without leading to a full ceasefire, while low-intensity attritional fighting is likely to continue along the front.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 8px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Most Likely Scenario:</em> The most likely short-term scenario is a continuation of the current state where negotiations proceed alongside continued fighting -- localized humanitarian truces and prisoner exchanges will likely occur, but a comprehensive peace settlement within 2026 appears unlikely.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Medium Term (1-3 years):</strong> In the medium term, with neither side able to achieve decisive military victory, analysts increasingly point to a transition toward a 'frozen conflict' along an effective de facto ceasefire line.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 8px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Expected Inflection Point:</em> Whether the US Congress and NATO approve the 15-50 year security guarantee framework, and whether Ukraine holds a national referendum on any settlement, are likely to be the pivotal turning points for the war's next phase.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Long Term Structural Trajectory:</strong> In the long term, European security discussions increasingly reference the possibility of an entrenched de facto boundary resembling the Korean DMZ, with Ukraine remaining outside NATO but backed by a 'quasi-alliance' system of bilateral Western security guarantees.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 12px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Structural Changes:</em> The war is structurally accelerating Europe's push to build indigenous defense-industrial capacity and reduce dependence on Russian energy, which is in turn expanding market opportunities in Europe for non-European defense exporters, including South Korea.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="background-color: #F1F5F9; border-left: 3px solid #64748B; padding: 10px 14px; margin-bottom: 24px; font-size: 12px; color: #334155;">
    <strong>Key Geopolitical Uncertainties:</strong>
    <ol style="margin: 4px 0 0 18px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 2px;">Fundamental disagreement persists over core issues like control of the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant and where a territorial freeze line would be drawn, meaning negotiations could collapse again at any point.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 2px;">Reports of a possible additional 30,000 North Korean troop deployment to Russia raise the risk that, if realized, consequences would extend beyond the battlefield into deepening North Korea-Russia military cooperation affecting the Korean Peninsula's security landscape.</li>
      <li>It remains unclear whether the US Congress and European parliaments will approve the proposed security guarantee framework, and whether NATO members will sustain the fiscal commitment required for the PURL arms-supply mechanism long-term.</li>
    </ol>
  </div>

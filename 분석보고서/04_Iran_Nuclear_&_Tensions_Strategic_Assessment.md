    1. BACKGROUND AND SCOPE
  </h2>
  <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px;">
    Building on distrust left from Trump's 2018 JCPOA withdrawal, the issue evolved through letter diplomacy and Oman-mediated indirect talks, two rounds of direct US military strikes, and the killing of Iran's Supreme Leader -- transforming from a nonproliferation dispute into a full-scale crisis over regime survival and the regional order.
  </p>
  <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <strong>Impact of the Trump Administration:</strong> The Trump administration combined maximum-pressure sanctions and letter diplomacy with two rounds of military strikes (June 2025, Feb 2026), insists on 'zero enrichment' as a red line, and restricted reconstruction funds to food and medicine purchases rather than state investment -- a consistently unilateral, transactional approach.
  </p>

  <h2 style="font-size: 15px; font-weight: 900; color: #0F172A; border-bottom: 1.5px solid #CBD5E1; padding-bottom: 4px; margin: 26px 0 10px 0; letter-spacing: 0.5px; text-transform: uppercase;">
    3. ECONOMIC AND SECURITY IMPACT ANALYSIS
  </h2>
  
  <div style="margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Security & Defense Dimension</div>
    <ul style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 4px 0 8px 20px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>North Korea & Regional Security:</strong> The Ghadr and Emad missiles Iran fired at Israel derive from North Korean-transferred Nodong (Shahab-3) and Scud-based designs from the 1980s-90s; experts say 'Iran would have been decisively defeated without North Korean support' -- meaning North Korean missile technology has now been combat-tested, an indirect but real proliferation implication for assessing DPRK missile threats.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Alliance Impact:</strong> With significant US military assets and diplomatic attention drawn to the Middle East, Korean security circles have raised concerns about the bandwidth available for strategic asset deployment and extended deterrence on the Korean Peninsula.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Military & Missile Threats:</strong> Evidence that combat-tested North Korean-derived missile technology has been upgraded (in range and accuracy) through Iranian use is drawing attention from Korean defense authorities as real-world performance data relevant to assessing North Korea's missile threat to the South.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div style="margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Economic, Trade & Financial Dimension</div>
    <ul style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 4px 0 8px 20px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Trade & Tariffs:</strong> Korean builders' Middle East contract value fell 90% year-on-year in Jan-May 2026 (to about $560 million, the region's share of overseas orders dropping from 60.4% to 14.6%), but firms like Samsung E&A and DL E&C could capture a share of up to $140 billion in projects over the next three years if reconstruction proceeds.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Foreign Exchange & Currency:</strong> The won weakened past 1,500 per dollar -- levels last seen during past economic crises -- as oil price spikes combined with safe-haven demand.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Cross-Border Investment:</strong> The OECD cut Korea's 2026 growth forecast from 2.1% to 1.7% in just three months, a sharper downgrade than Japan (0.9%), China (4.4%) or the US (2.0%), reflecting the drag from Korea's energy-intensive manufacturing base (petrochemicals, steel, semiconductors).</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Public Opinion & Expert Consensus</div>
    <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 0 0 4px 0;"><strong>Public Sentiment:</strong> A 2026 hrcopinion survey found 86% of Koreans are following the war closely, with 92% saying it affects the economy and 79% saying it affects national security; only 26% viewed the US-Israeli operation as justified, while 63% called it excessive -- indicating predominantly critical public sentiment.</p>
    <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 0;"><strong>Expert Assessment:</strong> Korean economists note that Korea is hit harder than Japan, China or the US by the same shock because of its structural dependence (70% of crude, 20% of LNG via the Middle East/Hormuz) and per-capita energy consumption 44% above the OECD average, compounded by refineries optimized for heavy crude that make rapid source diversification difficult.</p>
  </div>

  <!-- 7. Section 4: RISK OUTLOOK AND UNCERTAINTIES -->
  <h2 style="font-size: 15px; font-weight: 900; color: #0F172A; border-bottom: 1.5px solid #CBD5E1; padding-bottom: 4px; margin: 26px 0 10px 0; letter-spacing: 0.5px; text-transform: uppercase;">
    4. PERFORMANCE BENCHMARKS AND GAP ANALYSIS
  </h2>
  <div style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin-bottom: 12px;">
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Short Term (3-6 months):</strong> Intermittent low-level attacks around Hormuz and US retaliatory strikes are likely to continue for months, keeping oil prices volatile, with only limited prospects for formal talks resuming under Qatari-Pakistani mediation.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 8px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Most Likely Scenario:</em> The most likely near-term scenario is an unstable stalemate -- neither full war nor peace -- with Hormuz shipping partially restored (roughly half of prewar levels) alongside sporadic attacks.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Medium Term (1-3 years):</strong> Whether the technical working groups (nuclear, sanctions, dispute resolution) envisioned in the June MOU resume will determine if $30-50 billion in oil/gas infrastructure repair and a proposed up to $300 billion reconstruction fund move forward.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 8px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Expected Inflection Point:</em> A verifiable nuclear agreement and full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz mark the medium-term pivot point -- success would shift Middle East construction and energy markets into a reconstruction boom, while failure risks a prolonged war of attrition.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Long Term Structural Trajectory:</strong> RAND and other experts assess that Iran's political system has been fundamentally altered by Khamenei's death and could take years or decades to restabilize, with Iran's proxy network (Hezbollah, etc.) structurally weakened while Israel-Arab relations may realign.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 12px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Structural Changes:</em> The crisis is shifting the regional balance of power away from the Iran axis toward the Gulf states and Israel, while expanding the diplomatic influence of mediators like Oman and Qatar.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="background-color: #F1F5F9; border-left: 3px solid #64748B; padding: 10px 14px; margin-bottom: 24px; font-size: 12px; color: #334155;">
    <strong>Key Geopolitical Uncertainties:</strong>
    <ol style="margin: 4px 0 0 18px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 2px;">Regime stability: whether Mojtaba Khamenei, installed in a break from the anti-hereditary principle, can sustain the governance structure his father built over 36 years is a key variable for Iran's future posture.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 2px;">Ceasefire durability: two prior truces (April and June 2026) have both collapsed, and with Israel not party to the MOU, local clashes could reignite full-scale war at any time.</li>
      <li>Nuclear material and verification: Iran's refusal to disclose the location of its 60%-enriched uranium stockpile has broken IAEA 'continuity of knowledge,' leaving unclear whether a verifiable deal is achievable or hidden material could be reweaponized.</li>
    </ol>

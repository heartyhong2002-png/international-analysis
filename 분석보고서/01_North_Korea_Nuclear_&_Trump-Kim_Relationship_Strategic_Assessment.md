  <h2 style="font-size: 15px; font-weight: 900; color: #0F172A; border-bottom: 1.5px solid #CBD5E1; padding-bottom: 4px; margin: 26px 0 10px 0; letter-spacing: 0.5px; text-transform: uppercase;">
    1. BACKGROUND AND SCOPE
  </h2>
  <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px;">
    The North Korean nuclear and missile issue is the central variable in Trump's second-term North Korea policy, a phase in which the possibility of renewed leader-to-leader contact (absent since the 2019 Hanoi summit collapse) coexists with North Korea's continued consolidation of de facto nuclear-weapons-state status.
  </p>
  <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <strong>Impact of the Trump Administration:</strong> Trump attempted personal diplomacy from early in his term by drawing on his past rapport with Kim, but even a letter he sent was reportedly refused by North Korea in 2025, and subsequent overtures have remained limited to social-media messaging and symbolic exercise reductions rather than a formal summit, leaving unclear whether this translates into substantive policy change.
  </p>

    3. ECONOMIC AND SECURITY IMPACT ANALYSIS
  </h2>
  
  <div style="margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Security & Defense Dimension</div>
    <ul style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 4px 0 8px 20px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>North Korea & Regional Security:</strong> North Korea has simultaneously advanced its conventional and nuclear forces -- unveiling a nuclear-powered submarine, reinforcing its destroyer fleet, and diversifying missile tests -- with Kim Jong Un inspecting a new nuclear power facility in June 2026 and ordering an 'exponential' expansion of nuclear weapons production.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Alliance Impact:</strong> The US-South Korea alliance was reaffirmed as 'solid' at the August 2025 summit, but carries latent friction points, including tariffs imposed outside the existing free trade agreement, demands for higher defense cost-sharing, and US requests for troop repositioning aimed at countering China.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Military & Missile Threats:</strong> Between 2025 and 2026, North Korea repeatedly tested ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and a suspected submarine-launched weapon, notably continuing launches even immediately after Trump's October 2025 visit and his August 2026 conciliatory gestures, underscoring the persistence of the military threat to South Korea.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div style="margin-bottom: 14px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Economic, Trade & Financial Dimension</div>
    <ul style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 4px 0 8px 20px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Trade & Tariffs:</strong> The US-South Korea tariff agreement reached in July 2025 (a 15% tariff, plus $350 billion in investment and $100 billion in LNG purchase commitments) is already being implemented on a separate trade track from the nuclear issue, though it intersects with pressure to raise Seoul's defense cost-sharing (reportedly targeting a rise from 2.6% to 3.8% of GDP), shaping the broader alliance negotiation landscape.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Foreign Exchange & Currency:</strong> Recent won weakness has been attributed primarily to Middle East (Iran) tensions, rising oil prices, and US Federal Reserve policy rather than North Korea-specific risk, suggesting the nuclear issue functions as a latent background factor in the 'Korea discount' rather than a clearly identifiable direct trigger for short-term currency moves.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 4px;"><strong>Cross-Border Investment:</strong> With geopolitical uncertainty not fully resolved, implementation of the large US-South Korea investment pledge ($350 billion) is proceeding, and the trajectory of security risk is cited as a factor that could affect the pace of execution for key projects in semiconductors, shipbuilding, and batteries.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
    <div style="font-size: 13px; font-weight: 700; color: #1E293B; margin-bottom: 4px;">■ Public Opinion & Expert Consensus</div>
    <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 0 0 4px 0;"><strong>Public Sentiment:</strong> The combination of continued North Korean provocations and Trump's unpredictable personal diplomacy appears to have generated both hope for renewed dialogue and concern about security gaps within South Korean public discourse.</p>
    <p style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin: 0;"><strong>Expert Assessment:</strong> Experts such as Ankit Panda of the Carnegie Endowment argue that real progress requires dropping 'denuclearization' as a negotiating precondition in favor of a 'stable coexistence' framework, while other analysts point to the administration's policy incoherence and weak follow-through as the more fundamental constraint.</p>
  </div>

  <!-- 7. Section 4: RISK OUTLOOK AND UNCERTAINTIES -->
  <h2 style="font-size: 15px; font-weight: 900; color: #0F172A; border-bottom: 1.5px solid #CBD5E1; padding-bottom: 4px; margin: 26px 0 10px 0; letter-spacing: 0.5px; text-transform: uppercase;">
    4. PERFORMANCE BENCHMARKS AND GAP ANALYSIS
  </h2>
  <div style="font-size: 13px; color: #334155; margin-bottom: 12px;">
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Short Term (3-6 months):</strong> Through the end of 2026, a recurring pattern of intermittent North Korean missile tests alongside Trump's conciliatory gestures is likely to continue, with the key question being whether working-level contacts advance rather than whether a formal summit occurs.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 8px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Most Likely Scenario:</em> The outcome of the anticipated September Putin-Kim summit and North Korea's response to the US-South Korea-Japan Freedom Edge joint exercise (September 7-11) are likely to be key variables shaping the tension level over the following months.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Medium Term (1-3 years):</strong> Analysts at the Carnegie Endowment note that the alignment of North Korea's Workers' Party Congress (expected early 2026) and Trump's State of the Union address creates a potential window for the US to shift from a 'denuclearization' precondition to a 'stable coexistence' framework, which could reopen the door to dialogue.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 8px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Expected Inflection Point:</em> Experts identify whether the US drops 'complete denuclearization' as a precondition in favor of a risk-reduction and arms-control-centered approach as the key inflection point for any future shift in the situation.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 6px 0;"><strong>Long Term Structural Trajectory:</strong> Many experts, citing the failures of the past four US administrations, argue that only sustained engagement and diplomacy -- not pressure alone -- has ever constrained North Korea's nuclear program, and that how the second Trump administration wields this lesson will shape the long-term security architecture of the Korean Peninsula.</p>
    <p style="margin: 0 0 12px 0; color: #1E40AF;"><em>▶ Structural Changes:</em> The entrenchment of North Korea's de facto nuclear-weapons-state status alongside deepening alignment with Russia and China is seen as a structural shift that demands a fundamental redesign of the traditional denuclearization-based negotiating framework.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="background-color: #F1F5F9; border-left: 3px solid #64748B; padding: 10px 14px; margin-bottom: 24px; font-size: 12px; color: #334155;">
    <strong>Key Geopolitical Uncertainties:</strong>
    <ol style="margin: 4px 0 0 18px; padding: 0;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 2px;">Whether a Trump-Kim summit actually materializes remains uncertain as long as the gap persists between Trump's personal conciliatory gestures (exercise reductions, expressed willingness to meet) and the State Department's official 'complete denuclearization' stance.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 2px;">If North Korea-Russia military cooperation is institutionalized through a 2027-2031 five-year pact and large-scale additional troop deployments, North Korea's incentive to negotiate with the US could weaken while China's leverage over Pyongyang further erodes.</li>
      <li>It is unclear whether the Trump administration can sustain political momentum for a coherent North Korea policy amid competing foreign-policy priorities, notably tensions in the Middle East involving Iran.</li>
    </ol>
